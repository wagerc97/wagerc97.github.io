Computational methods for scientific questions.

I develop machine-learning, optimization, and software methods that make scientific prediction more useful, efficient, and reliable.

Research direction

I work at the intersection of scientific computing, machine learning, and optimization. My research uses permanent magnets as a demanding application area for developing methods that can support well-founded decisions from complex and limited data.

At the Christian Doppler Laboratory for magnet design through physics-informed machine learning, I work on data-mining routines, predictive models, and their validation in collaboration with project partners at CNRS and Toyota.

How I approach the problem

Design tasks often involves competing objectives, limited data, and expensive evaluations such as real world experiments or high-fidelity simulations. I analyse, augment and curate datasets, develop machine learning models to find patterns and to explore design trade-offs with optimization algorithms. ML models are also useful to replace experiments when direct evalution is impractical due to resource constraints. We need reliable models to support decision-making.

So, a central question is whether a model can be trusted where it is used. My work therefore evaluates uncertainty alongside prediction error. Incorporating uncertainty-aware models into optimization workflows offers a key advantage: We can constrain the confidence of predicted designs. This helps distinguish bad candidate designs from promising solutions.
